Your statutory rights

For goods sold to consumers in Ireland, the Consumer Rights Act 2022 and other applicable law provide rights where goods are faulty, not as described, unfit for purpose or otherwise not in conformity with the contract. These rights are not replaced or reduced by any manufacturer's commercial warranty.

Short-term right to terminate

Generally, if a lack of conformity becomes apparent and is reported within 30 days after receiving the goods, the consumer may have a short-term right to terminate the contract and receive a refund, subject to the conditions and exceptions in the Act. A consumer may choose repair or replacement instead. For perishable goods with a shorter expected life, the period can differ.

Repair, replacement and further remedies

After the short-term period, the consumer generally may choose a repair or replacement, subject to feasibility and proportionality. The remedy must be free of charge, within a reasonable time and without significant inconvenience. If a remedy cannot be provided, fails or the fault is sufficiently serious, a proportionate price reduction or termination may be available. For a lawful termination due to faulty goods, the trader ordinarily bears return costs.

Time limits and evidence

Consumer remedies can remain available for up to six years where goods could reasonably be expected to last that long; this is not a promise that every item lasts six years. During the first year, the trader generally bears the burden of proving that the goods conformed when delivered, subject to statutory exceptions; after that, evidence requirements may differ.

Commercial guarantees

Where a manufacturer or seller separately provides a commercial guarantee, its scope, duration, guarantor and conditions should be stated in the guarantee document. It is additional to, not a replacement for, statutory rights. We do not create a separate six-year commercial guarantee by describing the statutory limitation period.
